Our Imitations

Wilde's "most people are other people," revisited: imitation as apprenticeship for the child and the artist, and as a danger to originality in the age of AI.

Wasn't it Oscar Wilde who said, "Most people are other people, their thoughts are someone else's opinions, their lives a mimicry, their passions a quotation"? How true these words are, especially in this age of social media domination, when every moment of our life is controlled and regulated by the visual and audible media. Except for a small minority, most people belong to the category of imitators. They are copies of other people they imitate other people's thoughts, habits, opinions, style and are the carbon copies of those they imitate.

Why is it that most of us, instead of thinking for ourselves, turn elsewhere! Imitation is an integral aspect of our mind. As soon as a child becomes aware of his surroundings, that is by the time it reaches the late first year milestone, the imagination also starts functioning and over the years it gets stronger. The people around him are the objects of his imitation at first, then slowly it widens and finally embraces everything around it. It is a learning process and enriches its’ mental activities. By imitating, first the parents, then teachers and later on the people around, the child learns language, manners, skills and habits. Imitation is the best teacher for a child in the early years of his life.

Imitation can be of help in every stage of one's life. In sports, music or studies, one can improve one's skill by watching experts and copying them. Watching Messi perform will be an invaluable learning experience for a young footballer. Listening to a Maestro like Beethoven will open so many doors to a young musician. Copying positive role models like teachers can improve our behaviour and character.

Most artists and writers have started out by imitating earlier eminent painters and writers. Legendary artists like Pablo Picasso, Vincent Van Gogh, began their artistic career in this way. In art, history, copying earlier painters was a kind of primary school training. It is said that even Leonardo da Vinci, Michael Angelo and Raphael studied and copied each other. Writers have also followed this practice of imitating their predecessors. Yeats, the great Irish poet started out by imitating Keats. Most of Shakespeare's plays were modelled on earlier ones. A writer often imitates a great Master, to learn style, rhythm, and sentence structure before fashioning one of his own. Imitation often is an apprenticeship for artists and writers and has innumerable benefits.

But, like everything under the sun, imitation also has its shortcomings. It is true that it serves us in many ways, but the greatest danger of imitation is that it stifles originality and fosters mental sloth. Originality and creativity are two priceless gifts of the brain. As the Bard said," From airy nothing it can create a local habitation and a name." Everything that we take pride in, all the great books that have enlightened us, all the great pictures that have taken us to sublime heights of joy, all the great music that filled our hearts with celestial bliss - all this is the result of man's originality and creativity. When man neglects this, ignores this, he is ignoring this heavenly gift, which can raise him to unbelievable heights of achievement and self fulfilment. Everybody has these gifts to some degree by turning a blind eye to this, one is losing so much.

Why is it that we so often turn to imitations and second hand thinking instead of trying to find answers for ourselves. The main reason I would say is sloth, the desire to take the easy way out, the reluctance to take trouble. The tremendous advance made by online devices has only made the situation worse. Students nowadays seldom take the trouble to work out problems, prepare essays or dissertations by themselves. When AI is there, why trouble yourself. Chat GPT is there to hand everything on a platter. The problem is all answers, all studies look like mass produced factory goods - there is nothing of the man who is supposed to have created it in them, reminds you of the mass-produced children in Aldous Huxley's Brave New World. If the answer is already worked out and ready for you, why bother to go through the trouble of doing the problem yourself. This way you are putting your brain permanently in cold storage. This will eventually result in losing your originality and creativity, you will no longer be able to create new ideas in a style of your own. Indiscriminate copying may also result in undesirable things. There is also the danger of losing your self-confidence, if you are always depending on others for everything and never chalk out a path for yourself. Imitation is a powerful antibiotic; it has to be taken with caution.

Fashion is a field where imitation reveals its best as well as its worst aspects. Designers use their creativity and originality to bring out new eye-catching designs, once they are out, once the new season begins, it becomes the rage and every woman who wants to look fashionable and up to date follows the trends blindly, without bothering to find out whether they are suitable or not for them. Some designs may look lovely and fascinating on the tall, slim models who display them, but before rushing out to get these eye-catching dresses, one should pause and reflect whether it will suit you. It is better to remember that what is sauce for the goose is not sauce for the gander.

Jacqueline Kennedy was one of the most admired women of her times. Her tall elegant figure, her severely cut dresses, and straight hairstyle made her a fashion icon and women all over the world admired and tried to imitate her. There was a cartoon in the Readers Digest once which showed fat women, lean women, tall women - all trying to emulate her inimitable style. What looked admirable on Jacqueline Kennedy looked ridiculous and funny on them. When choosing fashions or trying to imitate somebody one has to be careful, and select wisely and carefully, otherwise you will cut a sorry figure.

Imitation like many other things is a double-edged weapon; it can benefit us in a thousand ways, especially in these days of AI and chat GPT. Positive emulation can be of great help, mentally as well as morally. It can raise us to new heights of knowledge and achievement, but it also holds many threats. The greatest danger of blindness imitation is that it results in the loss of our originality and stifles our creativity. The factors that make 'ourself' whatever we are or what is our essence is slowly destroyed. We become the "Hollow Men" who have lost our inner essence and have become pale reflections of others.
